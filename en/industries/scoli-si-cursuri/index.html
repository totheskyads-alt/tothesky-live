What needs to be clear before enrolment

Someone looking for a course is often trying to address a specific situation: communicating at work, preparing for an exam or entering a new field. When marketing courses, we start with that situation and explain who the offer is for. General statements about personal development do little to help readers decide whether a course suits them.

The ad can introduce a clear need, and the page should develop the conversation. We explain the activities included and how learning takes place. If prior knowledge or a level assessment is required, we say so before someone completes a form. We do not present attendance as a guarantee of employment, promotion or a qualification.

For children's courses, parents need to understand how the school communicates with families and how they can follow their child's participation. For adults, missed lesson arrangements, access to materials or compatibility with work may matter. We include these details only when they reflect the actual course conditions.

We make the contact step specific. Readers should know whether they are asking a question, requesting an assessment or starting enrolment. We explain what happens after submission and what information the team will provide. This prepares a relevant conversation without implying that completing a form automatically confirms a place.

How we advertise courses during enrolment

Google Search can suit prospective learners already looking for a particular course, study format or school nearby. We build campaigns around those intentions and direct people to the relevant offer. We separate searches for different courses so the message answers the expressed need and the team can understand what generates enquiries.

Performance Max may be worth testing when the conversion objective is clearly defined and relevant materials are available. Before using it, we check which action is being reported as a result. A course page visit is not an enrolment enquiry. Campaign choice needs to reflect the action you want people to take and the quality of measurement.

On Facebook and Instagram, we can show how teaching works through lesson excerpts, teacher explanations or answers to common questions. Lead campaigns can start conversations with interested people, while the form needs to provide enough course context. For children's courses, communication addresses the parents making the decision.

We align the advertising calendar with actual class availability. When enrolment closes, we update messages and destinations. TikTok may be considered for educational content suited to the audience where production resources exist. We do not invent urgency or advertise places the school cannot confirm.

How we follow enquiries through to enrolment

For school and course advertising, form submissions are only the start of the assessment. We want to know how many enquiries are valid, how many people respond and how many move towards enrolment. We agree on what each stage means so that a timetable question is not reported as a completed enrolment. This makes the results easier to interpret.

We track cost per enquiry and, where the necessary information is available, cost per confirmed enrolment. We compare these by course, campaign and period. A campaign may generate inexpensive enquiries for a level the school does not offer. Another may bring fewer contacts who are more suited to the available course. The team's conversations reveal that difference.

That is why we need simple, consistent feedback: valid contact, requested course, format suitability and enrolment status. We do not request personal details that add nothing to the marketing assessment. If unsuitable enquiries are common, we review messages, forms and search terms before changing the budget.

We also allow for decision time. Someone enquiring now may return after speaking with family or clarifying their schedule. Our reporting separates recent enquiries from those with a final outcome, so a delayed decision is not automatically mistaken for a lack of interest.

Expert English: enrolment enquiries from Google

For Expert English, an English language school, we worked with Google campaigns focused on enrolment enquiries. The case study reports steady enrolment enquiries at an average cost of ~16 $ per enquiry, with a reported best cost of 6,67 $ on Performance Max. For your school, we structure campaigns by course, level and format, with pages dedicated to the advertised offer.

When planning your advertising, we start with the questions that shape course choice: does it fit around work, what level is required and when does the class start? On the destination page, we present schedules, level assessment and class availability. When responding to an enquiry, the team explains suitable options and the next step towards enrolment.

The practical lesson is to define the objective clearly before launch. Decide whether you want information requests, level assessments or enrolment enquiries, and make sure the page communicates the same action. Then prepare how the team will record and follow up with contacts. Record the requested course, preferred time slot and outstanding questions so the team can continue the conversation with relevant information.

We use the case study to discuss what needs to be measured and tested. In the weekly report, we track cost per enquiry, contacts followed up and their progress towards enrolment. We start with your offer, intended audience and enrolment period, then assess campaign results in context.
