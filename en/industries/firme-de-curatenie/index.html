What customers need to know before requesting a quote

When someone compares cleaning companies, the service name is not always enough. Cleaning after renovation, regular home cleaning and cleaning commercial premises involve different expectations. If everything sits under the same general promise, customers have to guess what they can request and what they will receive.

On the website, we explain the tasks included and the situations that need a separate discussion. You can clarify whether your team brings cleaning products, how you handle specialist surfaces and what needs preparing before arrival. These details help people compare actual services without turning every enquiry into a long exchange of basic questions.

Photographs from completed jobs can support the explanation when they are authentic and published with permission. We show context, rather than just a spotless corner: the type of property, the work completed and any limitations. Relevant reviews can add to the picture, particularly when they describe communication and how requirements were handled.

Advertising for cleaning companies should prepare customers for that same experience. If you advertise cleaning after a move, the landing page should explain that service. We make the quote request clear about what happens next. Customers know whether you will contact them for details or to arrange a visit, and you receive an enquiry with more useful context.

How we choose between Meta ads and Google searches

On Facebook and Instagram, we can show a specific cleaning job to people in your service area. For cleaning company promotion, we propose enquiry campaigns using real images and messages about an available service. Content about preparing a home for a move has a different context from content about maintaining an office.

An advert's enquiry form can be an option when the offer is straightforward. If the work needs more explanation, we send people to a dedicated page. The decision depends on more than the number of forms received. We also consider how well people understand the service and how easily you can continue the conversation.

Google Search is worth assessing when there are relevant searches for your services and location. A campaign can respond to an existing need, such as cleaning after renovation. We review search terms and exclude unsuitable intentions, including job searches or cleaning products where these are outside your offer.

We also choose channels around your ability to take on work. We do not promote a service when you have no team available to deliver it. We test distinct messages, review enquiry quality and adjust budget allocation as we learn which customers contact you. Video can support the campaign when it explains the process clearly and respects the privacy of the spaces being filmed.

What we track from enquiry to accepted job

A completed form can represent very different situations. Someone may need a job outside your service area, while someone else is looking for a service you do not offer. In marketing for cleaning companies, we assess enquiries by the type of work, location and frequency requested.

We establish a simple way to follow what happens after contact. Could the request be assessed? Did you send a quote? Did the customer accept the job? If the conversation stopped, we record the reason when it is known. We track response time, availability for the requested date and objections about pricing or included tasks to identify what needs clarification.

In reporting, we separate cost per enquiry from cost per accepted job when there is enough data. We examine which services people request and whether campaigns attract occasional jobs or recurring work. We group enquiries, quotes and accepted jobs by their originating campaign so you can see which messages attract the services you want to grow.

Your feedback informs our campaign decisions. If you receive requests for work you do not undertake, we clarify the message. If people repeatedly need the same explanation before accepting a quote, we improve the page. If suitable enquiries go unanswered, we discuss how they are handled before increasing advertising. Measurement should show you where a change is needed.

Cleanix: what you can learn from the Meta campaign

For Cleanix, a cleaning company in Chicago, we worked with Meta advertising to generate customer enquiries. The case study records real customer enquiries, at ~12 $ each, from the first month on Meta. The first month showed growth.

For your campaign, we propose distinct messages for one-off cleaning and recurring maintenance. The form asks for property type, size, location and the preferred date. The page explains the tasks included and how pricing is determined, helping customers understand the offer and provide the details needed to assess the job.

For your business, the practical lesson is to build promotion around a service you can explain and deliver clearly. Choose the type of work you want to attract, prepare authentic material and decide which information you need for a quote. An enquiry becomes easier to assess when the advert and form address the same need.

In your weekly report, we propose tracking cost per enquiry, requests that reach the quote stage and accepted jobs, grouped by service and advert message. We also record questions about cleaning products, access to the property and scheduling. We use these findings to improve your page, prepare your team's answers and direct the budget towards the types of work you want to attract.
