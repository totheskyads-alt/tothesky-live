What ROAS means and how to calculate it

ROAS stands for Return on Ad Spend and measures revenue attributed to advertising relative to the amount spent on ads. The formula is simple: ROAS = revenue from ads / ad spend. It tells you how much revenue you generate for every RON spent, but it does not tell you how much profit stays in your business.

Example: you spend 1,000 RON on ads and generate 5,000 RON in revenue attributed to them. Divide 5,000 by 1,000 and you get a ROAS of 5. You can write the same result as 5x or 500%. Each version means the same thing: every RON spent generated 5 RON in reported revenue.

For a useful calculation, use the same period and currency for both amounts. Do not divide your entire store's revenue by the budget of a single campaign. Those figures describe different activities. If you take revenue from Meta, compare it with the corresponding spend in Meta.

In the accounts we manage, we read ROAS alongside revenue and spend. A good ratio from a small number of orders cannot tell you how much room your store has to grow. Enter your spend, attributed revenue and margin in the calculator above. It estimates your ROAS, minimum threshold and amount left after ads. If you have no ad spend, this formula cannot calculate ROAS.

Minimum ROAS: how to find your break-even point

Minimum ROAS, also called break-even ROAS, is the ratio at which the margin from sales exactly covers your ad spend. The formula is minimum ROAS = 1 / margin, with margin written as a decimal. Example: with a 50% margin, calculate 1 / 0.50 to get a threshold of 2.

Here, margin means the share of your selling price left after product costs, shipping paid by you and payment processing fees. Calculate it before subtracting advertising costs. Do not confuse margin with markup: margin is calculated relative to the selling price, while markup is calculated relative to the product cost.

Example: with a 25% margin, your threshold is 4. Example: with a 20% margin, your threshold is 5. The less you keep from each order before advertising, the more revenue you need from the same budget. The table provides further examples; displayed figures may be rounded.

If your products have different margins, use an average margin weighted by sales revenue. This means products generating more revenue have a greater influence on the calculation. At break-even, you have covered the variable costs included in your margin and your ads. You have not automatically covered salaries, rent, subscriptions or agency fees. Your business needs room above this threshold to make a profit.

What is a good ROAS for an online store?

A good ROAS starts with your margin. If your result exceeds the break-even threshold, money remains after the variable costs included and your advertising spend. How good that result is then depends on the amount left, your fixed costs and your order volume. The same ROAS can leave money in one store and generate losses in another.

In the accounts we manage, we reviewed online stores running Meta ads in RON over the last 90 days, from 26 August to 23 November 2026. Across 7 established stores, Meta-reported ROAS ranged from 3.8x to 12x, with a median of approximately 5.8x. The median is the middle value when you put the results in order.

These figures represent revenue attributed to ads divided by ad spend. They are a reference from our accounts, not a universal target or verified profit for each store.

When we review an account, we compare its result with its margin and stage of development. We also check whether the ads generate enough volume for the amount left to matter. We do not recommend treating our median as your target. First establish what your advertising needs to cover, then assess the result.

Why platform ROAS is not your profit

An advertising platform shows the revenue it attributes to ads, not the money left after all your costs. Attribution is the rule a platform uses to give an ad credit for an order. An attributed sale does not, by itself, prove that the ad caused the purchase.

Under Meta's default 7-day click and 1-day view window, an order can be attributed to an ad if the person clicked within the previous 7 days or saw the ad within the previous day. Check the actual setting in your account before comparing reports, because results depend on the attribution rules being used.

This means platform ROAS can be higher than the figure calculated from orders your store attributes to that channel. Google Ads and Meta can both count the same order. Adding the revenue reported by both platforms risks counting the same money twice. Some orders also come from customers who would have purchased anyway.

Returns, cancellations and differences between reported revenue and money received create further gaps. We check reports against actual orders, then remove sales that were not retained. We compare values on a consistent basis, including how VAT and shipping are treated. ROAS remains useful for advertising decisions, but profit requires verified revenue and a calculation that subtracts your costs.

ROAS vs ROI vs POAS: what each tells you

ROAS answers the question: how much revenue did I generate for every RON spent on ads? It uses revenue without subtracting product costs or other expenses. It helps you assess advertising efficiency, provided you understand how sales are measured and what margins the purchased products carry.

ROI, or return on investment, compares the net gain from an investment with its cost. The formula is ROI = net gain / investment cost, multiplied by 100 to express it as a percentage. Before calculating it, decide which costs you are including. If you are reviewing your entire marketing activity, include the relevant expenses beyond the budget paid directly to advertising platforms.

POAS stands for Profit on Ad Spend and is calculated as gross profit from ads / ad spend. In this guide, we use gross profit before advertising, calculated after product costs, shipping paid by you and payment processing fees. We specify the basis because reports can use the same term for different calculations.

The calculator follows the same logic: revenue × margin / 100, then subtract ad spend. The result shows how much of your margin remains after advertising. We do not call it your company's net profit. In the accounts we manage, ROAS helps us assess campaign results, while margin tells us whether those sales leave money for the rest of the business.

A real example: ROAS for Regal Pet

Regal Pet is a pet supplies store featured in our public Google Ads case study. Account ROAS increased from 8.2x to approximately 13x, with peaks of 14-18x on Performance Max. Performance Max is a Google Ads campaign type that can serve ads across several Google channels.

In October 2026, the account recorded 17,179 RON in ad spend and 296,462 RON in conversion value reported by Google Ads. Dividing reported value by spend gives approximately 17x. Viewed the other way around, advertising spend represented approximately 5.8% of the revenue reported by the platform.

What can you learn from this? First, separate the result for the entire account from the peak achieved by an individual campaign. A Performance Max result does not automatically describe every campaign. Keep the reporting periods separate too: October's result does not replace the results from other periods in the study. Otherwise, accurate figures can still lead you to an inaccurate interpretation.

Next, consider what is missing from the report before calculating profit. Conversion value alone does not tell you product costs, how many orders were returned or the store's fixed costs. We use this example to show the relationship between spend and reported revenue. We do not turn it into a promise for another store. Your own threshold still starts with your margin.

When to increase your [advertising budget](/en/guides/cat-costa-reclamele-facebook/)

You can consider increasing your budget when ROAS stays above your threshold for several consecutive weeks and actual orders confirm the result. One good day is not enough. An attractive report is not useful if those sales are cancelled, returned or recorded with incorrect values.

Before increasing spend, we check whether the margin being used still reflects the products being sold. Discounts, shipping covered by the store or changes in the products customers buy can shift your threshold. We also check whether you have stock, can fulfil orders and have money available to replenish inventory. Ads can generate orders before the proceeds are available to fund your next supplier purchase.

Increase gradually and watch what happens after each change. Do not assume a larger budget will automatically maintain the same ROAS. Review spend, confirmed revenue and the amount left after variable costs and ads together. If the result moves close to your threshold, pause the increase and investigate the reason.

In the accounts we manage, the useful question is whether the additional budget leaves more money in the business. A slightly lower ROAS can be acceptable if volume grows and the amount left grows with it. More orders do not help if every order adds a loss. Keep the decision tied to your margin and actual orders.

Mistakes that lead you to misread ROAS

The first mistake is directly comparing stores with different margins. One store can afford advertising at a ratio that creates a loss for another. Before asking what ROAS someone else achieves, calculate how much you keep from an order. Then compare similar periods and measurement rules.

The second mistake is watching the ratio without considering volume. You can have a good ROAS and too little revenue to cover the rest of your business. You can also have a lower ratio but more money left after advertising. Always read the ratio alongside spend, order value and the margin on those orders.

The third mistake is accepting impossible figures. Check measurement before celebrating: the currency being sent, the order value and any duplicate records.

The fourth mistake is judging everything in the first week. You need time for orders to be confirmed and to see whether results repeat. That does not mean allowing an obvious problem to keep consuming budget. We investigate measurement errors and check orders immediately, then assess performance over a sufficient period. The calculator gives you a starting point; accurate data makes its result useful.

In short

A good ROAS exceeds the threshold calculated from your margin and is confirmed by actual orders. Use the calculator as a starting point, then check whether the amount left after advertising also covers fixed costs, agency fees and the profit you want to make.
