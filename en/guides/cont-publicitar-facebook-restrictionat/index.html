Facebook ad account restricted or disabled: what to do, step by step

To The Sky · 25 November 2026

If your Facebook ad account is restricted or disabled, first check Account Quality to see what is affected and which options are available. Then check your account information and request a review if that option is available. A review does not guarantee that access will be restored.

Identify the issue before requesting a review

What does a restricted Facebook ad account mean?

When you see an advertising issue in Meta, first identify exactly what has been restricted. It may be the ad account, your user account, a Page or the Business account. You need to distinguish between these situations before preparing an appeal. Otherwise, you may end up describing a different problem from the one shown in the platform.

A rejected ad is not the same as a disabled ad account. For a rejected ad, you can request another review of that ad in Account Quality. When the restriction concerns an account, check that account’s status and available options. Meta explains these distinctions in its information about advertising restrictions.

In the accounts we manage, we start with the exact message rather than assuming every interruption means the same thing. If the message concerns a profile, we do not treat it as an individual ad rejection. If it concerns the Business account, that is where we look.

For you, this distinction determines where to start. Note what is affected and how Meta describes the situation. That wording will help when you check the information and explain the issue in your review request. Avoid presenting a suspected cause as an established fact. For the big picture, read our complete guide to online ads for small businesses.

Where can you check the issue and Meta’s message?

When an ad account, user account, Page or Business account is restricted, you will usually receive an email and an in-platform notification. To check the status and available options, go to the Account Quality section in Meta. This is where you start clarifying what is happening with the account.

Read the full message. In client accounts, we have encountered this wording: “Your ad account was flagged because of unusual activity. All your ads have been paused. To restart them, contact Facebook to confirm your account information.” In this case, the account is disabled and ads are paused until the account information is confirmed.

The message asks you to confirm the information. By itself, it does not tell you which recent change triggered the situation. That is why we separate what Meta says from what we need to check in the account. A new payment or a new administrator is something to investigate, not a cause proven by this notification alone.

Keep the exact wording when preparing your explanation. If the interface looks different, check the Account Quality section in Meta for the affected item’s status and available options. Do not assume that every account shows the same options or that every message requires the same action.

What should you check before appealing a restriction?

Before submitting an appeal, we check what has changed recently. The aim is to understand the situation and prepare an accurate explanation. The list below describes our approach in the accounts we manage. It is not a list of causes confirmed by Meta for every restriction.

1. Check whether there has been a new payment and whether the payment method is valid and in the company’s name. 2. Check whether a new administrator has been added or whether there have been logins from another country. 3. Check for repeatedly rejected ads, a newly introduced domain or a product in a sensitive category. 4. Check that every administrator uses two-factor authentication and avoid personal accounts with no genuine activity. 5. Check the company details in the Business account and whether the domain is verified.

Do not assume the latest change automatically caused the restriction. If you added an administrator before the message appeared, you can mention that change as a fact. Do not claim that Meta restricted the account for that reason unless the message says so.

By the end, you should be able to explain clearly what you found and what you corrected. If something is unclear, verify it before including it. A coherent request starts with information you can support.

How do you request a review, step by step?

You can appeal a restriction by requesting a review. Start with Meta’s official guide to requesting a review and the options available in your account. The interface may change, so follow what Meta displays for the issue you have identified rather than expecting a particular button label.

1. Go to the Account Quality section in Meta and check the affected item’s status. 2. Read the restriction information and identify the review option, if one is available. 3. Prepare your explanation using the message you received, the checks you completed and accurate account information. 4. Follow the requirements Meta displays for your request and submit it through the available option. 5. Watch your email for the decision and check the status in Account Quality.

If the issue is a rejected ad, the request concerns a review of that ad. Do not describe the account as disabled if the information shown only concerns an individual ad. Keep that distinction clear in your explanation too.

Our check before submission is straightforward: the request describes the correct issue, the information is consistent and assumptions are not presented as facts. If no review option appears, consult the official guide and the options shown for your situation. Do not invent a procedure based on a different account.

What should you write, and what should you avoid during the review?

Write a short explanation focused on the account’s situation. Start with what is restricted and the message you received. Follow with the relevant checks and any corrections you made. There is no need to turn the request into a business presentation or a list of suspected causes.

You can use this writing structure: “I am requesting a review of the restriction applied to the ad account. The message displayed is: [exact message]. I have checked: [checks completed]. I have corrected: [corrections made, if any].” Include only accurate information. This is wording we suggest, not an official Meta form or a guarantee of approval.

If you received the unusual activity message, relate your explanation to the request to confirm the account information. Do not say all checks are complete if some details remain unclear. Do not describe an issue as resolved when you have only identified it.

During the review, we do not create new accounts to bypass the restriction. Doing so can make the situation worse. We follow the same rule when the campaigns are important to the business.

Avoid promises you cannot support. A clear explanation helps present the situation, but Meta makes the decision. The wording of a request cannot guarantee that the account will be restored.

How long does a review take, and what happens after the decision?

Meta says reviews are usually completed in approximately 48 hours, although some take longer. The decision arrives by email. This information appears in Meta’s review guidance. The 48-hour period is an approximate reference, not a guaranteed deadline for your account.

If that period has passed, it does not automatically mean your request was rejected. The official information makes clear that some reviews take longer. Equally, submitting a request does not mean the restriction will be lifted. Keep that distinction in mind when planning your work.

Once you receive the decision, read the email and check the current status in the Account Quality section in Meta. Base your next action on the decision and the available options. Do not assume a response about an individual ad also resolves a separate account or profile restriction.

If the restriction is lifted, return to your security, payment and Business account checks. If it remains, follow the information displayed for your situation. Do not treat a new account as a way around the restriction.

We communicate the submission of a review request separately from the receipt of a decision. This helps the business avoid basing its plans on account access that has not yet been confirmed.

How can you reduce the risk of another restriction?

In our day-to-day work, prevention means keeping accounts in order and knowing what changes we make. This is not a promise that Facebook advertising restrictions can be eliminated permanently. These are checks we use when managing accounts, including after a restriction has been lifted.

We start with security. We use two-factor authentication for every administrator and avoid personal accounts with no genuine activity. When a new administrator is added or we notice logins from another country, we include those changes in our checks. We do not automatically label them as the reason for a restriction.

We also check the payment method: it should be valid and in the company’s name. In the Business account, we keep company details in order and the domain verified. These practices are part of how we work, not a guarantee that Meta will approve an appeal.

We pay attention to repeatedly rejected ads, new domains and products in sensitive categories. When these arise, we examine them before drawing conclusions about an account issue.

We keep important campaigns in a clean account with its information and administration in order. This does not mean creating a new account to bypass an existing restriction. The rule remains the same: clarify the affected account’s issue and use the options Meta provides.

When does it make sense to ask for help?

Ask for help when you are unsure what has been restricted, cannot explain recent changes or find that the account information is not in order. The exact message from Meta is a useful starting point. From there, you can discuss the affected account, the checks completed and the review request in concrete terms.

At To The Sky, we are a young, dynamic team based in Malta, specialising in advertising and websites. In the accounts we manage, we start by identifying the issue and checking the information. We do not begin with a promise that every account can be restored, and we do not treat every restriction the same way.

To prepare for the conversation, note the message you received, the item affected and what changed recently. Add what you have already checked and whether you have submitted a review request. If a decision has arrived, use its exact wording rather than replacing it with an interpretation.

If you are wondering how to get your Facebook ad account unblocked, the practical answer starts with these details. Help can bring structure to the checks and explanation, but the decision remains with Meta. For us, a responsible approach means knowing what is confirmed, what still needs checking and which options are available for your situation.

In short

Check Account Quality to identify what is restricted, verify the account information and request a review through the available options.
