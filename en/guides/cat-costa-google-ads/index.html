How much does Google Ads cost in 2026? Real prices in RON

To The Sky · 25 November 2026

Across the 7 online stores whose Google Ads accounts we manage in RON (Romanian lei), cost per click ranged from 0.82 to 1.99 RON, while cost per order ranged from ~20 to ~53 RON. The data covers 26 August - 23 November 2026, with approximately 396,000 RON spent in total and more than 380,000 clicks. We are To The Sky, a young and dynamic team based in Malta specialising in advertising and websites, and this guide explains how to turn those figures into a budget for your business.

How much does a Google Ads click cost, and why does it vary?

A Google Ads click cost between 0.82 and 1.99 RON across the accounts analysed, with a median of ~1.15 RON. The median is the middle value when account results are arranged in order. This gives online stores a concrete reference point for an initial budget discussion.

For Search campaigns, you generally pay for a click rather than simply having your ad displayed. CPC means cost per click and tells you how much you pay when someone clicks your ad. The price is set through an auction and depends on competition, your bid, and the quality of your ad and landing page.

Ad Rank determines whether your ad is eligible to appear and its position. Quality Score is a diagnostic tool for relevance and quality, rather than a direct input into the auction. Google explains the distinction in its documentation on Ad Rank and Quality Score.

For your business, the useful question is how much that visitor is worth. Someone searching for a specific product has a different intention from someone exploring a broad category. We start our cost analysis with that intention and follow it through to orders. This helps us decide where to bid and which page should receive the visit. For the big picture, read our complete guide to online ads for small businesses.

What you actually pay for: clicks, impressions, orders and ROAS

When you ask how much advertising on Google costs, it helps to follow the complete journey: impression, click, order and sales value. Each metric answers a different question. Together, they show how your budget works and what your business receives in return.

Cost per 1,000 impressions, known as CPM, expresses spend relative to the visibility of your ads. Across our accounts, it ranged from ~16 to ~59 RON, with a median of ~32 RON. You can track this metric even when a campaign charges per click. Click-through rate, or CTR, is the percentage of impressions that lead to a click: between ~1.6% and ~3.9%, with a median of ~2.4%.

Cost per order divides advertising spend by reported orders. In our analysis, it ranged from ~20 to ~53 RON, with a median of ~36 RON. This metric directly connects your advertising investment with order volume.

ROAS divides sales revenue reported by Google Ads by advertising spend. Our range was ~6x to ~16x, with a median of ~7.6x. ROAS measures reported revenue relative to ad spend, rather than profit. For commercial decisions, we read it alongside product margins, delivery and other store costs. This helps you choose which products deserve more advertising investment.

How much monthly budget do you need for Google Ads?

A useful Google Ads budget starts with your sales objective. Decide how many orders you want to attract and what cost per order your margins can support. Then turn that objective into a monthly amount, distributed across your priority campaigns and products.

Example: if you want ~100 orders per month at an average cost of ~36 RON, you need around 3,600 RON per month. The calculation is straightforward: the number of orders multiplied by the average cost per order. As a rounded reference, that means a daily budget of ~120 RON. This planning example uses the median observed across our accounts.

Google uses an average daily budget. For most campaigns, it can spend up to 2 times that amount on a particular day. If your average daily budget remains unchanged, the monthly charging limit is 30.4 times your average daily budget. The rule is explained in Google's official documentation.

For an exact setting, divide the monthly amount by 30.4; the daily figure above is rounded. Our planning also considers store priorities: available stock, products with suitable margins and the capacity to process orders. This connects your investment with what you can sell and deliver, giving budget adjustments a clear commercial reason.

Search, Shopping and Performance Max: which fits your business?

Your campaign type affects where ads appear, how people reach you and how we assess cost. We start with what you sell and how customers search for it. Then we choose the format that can support your target for orders or enquiries.

Search means ads displayed in Google search results. It suits explicitly searched services and relevant searches for an online store. You can align the message with the service, category or product being searched for. Cost reflects the auctions for those searches and how well your offer matches customer intent.

Shopping presents products with an image, price and store name. For an online store, catalogue information helps shoppers assess the product before visiting the website. The catalogue submitted to Google, known as a product feed, needs clear and current information. See Google's explanation of Shopping.

Performance Max uses automation to distribute ads across multiple Google channels according to the campaign objective. It can support both online sales and service enquiries. Google explains the campaign's reach here.

We compare these campaigns through the orders, sales value and relevant enquiries they generate. CPC provides context, while commercial outcomes help us decide how to distribute the budget between them.

How much does a service enquiry cost?

For a service business, the objective is to bring interested people into a conversation with your team. A lead is someone who expresses interest through a form, a call or another agreed contact action. Cost per lead divides advertising spend by the number of those enquiries.

In our public case study for Expert English, an English language school, enrolment enquiries cost ~16 $ on average and from 6.67 $ through Performance Max. For Matter of Care, a home care service in Malta, enquiries from families cost ~15 € per contact. We retain the currencies used in the case studies.

These examples show how we measure advertising when the sales process continues after the initial contact. Every business has its own context, and these figures describe those specific projects. For your service business in Romania, we start with what makes an enquiry relevant, the area you serve and the value of a customer.

Our budget discussion also covers what happens after the form is submitted. Who responds, what information do they receive, and how does the enquiry become an appointment or a contract? Connecting advertising to this process lets you assess cost against real commercial opportunities. A clear form and a page that explains the service help people take the next step.

How to lower cost per click while protecting sales

To lower your Google Ads cost per click while keeping sales in focus, work on relevance. The ad, the search and the landing page should address the same need. Google explains that better ad quality can contribute to lower click costs in its ad quality documentation.

We start with the search terms bringing visitors to the website. Negative keywords are terms for which you choose not to show your ad. We use them to direct the budget towards searches that fit your offer. Their selection takes account of the products sold, buyer intent and the results already observed in the account.

Next comes the page: does it load quickly, present the product being searched for and make buying easy? Visitors should find clear information about price, availability, delivery and checkout. For services, the page should explain the offer and make sending a relevant enquiry straightforward.

We then organise advertising around products that sell and margins that support investment. Tracking, meaning the measurement of actions taken after an ad interaction, needs to record orders and their values correctly. We assess changes through cost per order, ROAS and sales volume. This keeps CPC reductions connected to the store's objective and directs the budget towards opportunities we can measure.

What does Google Ads management cost, and what should a quote include?

The total cost of advertising includes the budget paid to Google and the management service. The budget pays for ad delivery. Management covers the work involved in planning, setting up, monitoring and optimising campaigns. These components should be separate and easy to understand in a quote.

When comparing Google Ads management proposals, start with the scope. Ask for the initial analysis, campaign structure, search research, ad copy and conversion measurement checks to be specified. A conversion is the action you want to generate, such as an order or an enquiry.

For online stores, clarify whether the proposal includes work on the product feed and checks on reported order values. For services, establish how relevant forms and calls will be measured. The quote should also explain who prepares visual assets, who updates landing pages and which activities are contracted separately.

You also need a clear reporting schedule, access to the account and explanations of the decisions made. Our management discussions start with the business objective and the work needed to support it. This lets you assess a proposal through concrete responsibilities: what you receive, who handles it and how you follow progress. Ask for the terms of the engagement too, including how budget changes are approved.

What results can you expect in the first few months?

During the first few months, we build a useful evidence base for advertising decisions. We verify measurement, follow the searches and products generating orders, and observe how visitors respond to the offer. Learning means using these signals to guide campaigns more effectively towards the chosen objective.

Early findings help us prioritise. We identify which categories deserve support, which messages attract relevant interest and where the page could explain the offer more clearly. Optimisation means making adjustments based on measured results: budgets, ads, promoted products and the website experience.

Regal Pet, a pet supplies store, provides a public example of growth over time. Account ROAS increased from 8.2x to ~13x, with peaks of 14-18x on Performance Max. We read the overall account result separately from campaign peaks to maintain a clear view of progress.

For your store, we track order volume, cost per order and reported sales value together. For services, we follow relevant enquiries and their progression towards appointments or contracts. Each stage of our work should produce a clear decision: what to keep, what to improve and where to invest next. This means the first few months provide both measurable results and a more precise direction for your next budget.
