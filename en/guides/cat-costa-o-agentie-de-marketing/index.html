How Much Does a Marketing Agency Cost in 2026 for Facebook and Google Ads?

To The Sky · 25 November 2026

In 2026, agency management of Facebook and Instagram ads is estimated at RON 1,000-5,000 per month, separate from a minimum advertising budget of RON 2,100-9,000 per month, according to Brig.ro. For Google Ads, the same source gives a minimum budget of RON 2,100-3,000 per month, plus management. Almost 9 in 10 PPC agencies in the Clutch sample for Romania list hourly rates between $25 and $99. To choose an offer, you need to separate what you pay for the work, what you spend on advertising and what remains from sales. Below, we explain how to compare these costs for your business.

What you pay for

Marketing agency costs at a glance

For agency management of Facebook and Instagram ads, the 2026 benchmark published by Brig.ro is RON 1,000-5,000 per month. Separately, the source lists a minimum advertising budget of RON 2,100-9,000 per month. For Google Ads, the stated minimum budget is RON 2,100-3,000 per month, plus management. These are indicative market estimates, while our account data describes the period analysed.

Another perspective comes from Clutch. In November 2026, among the 43 Romanian PPC agencies displaying rates within the first 50 listings, 44% charge $25-49 per hour, 44% charge $50-99 per hour, 7% charge $100-149 per hour and 5% charge under $25. That puts almost 9 in 10 between $25 and $99 per hour.

An hourly rate gives you a benchmark, but it does not establish your monthly bill. You also need to know the workload and included services. Ask for a proposal that separates management, platform spending and any additional work.

Your advertising budget and agency fee are separate costs

Your advertising budget is the money spent on Facebook, Instagram or Google to distribute your ads. The agency fee covers campaign preparation, management and improvement, as defined in the proposal. When comparing offers, put these amounts on separate lines. Otherwise, you could compare a package that includes advertising spend with one that covers services alone.

Across our accounts, during the last 90 days analysed, 26 August-23 November 2026, Facebook and Instagram cost per click ranged from RON 0.40 to RON 2.62. You can find the data in our Facebook advertising cost guide. This metric shows what we paid the platform for a click. It does not represent the management fee or the cost of a sale.

For Google Ads during the same period, we recorded RON 0.82-1.99 per click and a median cost per order of approximately RON 36 for online stores. We explain the figures in our Google Ads cost guide. Median cost per order is a different metric from cost per click and should be read separately from the agency fee.

Ask for this separation in both the proposal and reporting. You should see platform spending, management fees and measured results. If landing pages or advertising materials are included, ask for them to be listed explicitly. This makes each payment clear and lets you track the total cost.

Payment models and when each makes sense

A fixed monthly retainer makes sense when you want predictable spending and have a clearly defined workload. The proposal should specify which platforms are managed, which materials are included and which activities cost extra. Check what happens when you add products, campaigns or a new market.

A percentage of advertising spend links the management fee to the amount invested in the platforms. It can make sense when expanding campaigns also creates more work. Ask how the fee is calculated and how services change as spending grows. Any budget increase should be discussed alongside results and your business's capacity.

A setup fee covers the initial work specified in the proposal. This may include account configuration, conversion tracking and campaign preparation. Ask for the deliverables and the criteria used to confirm completion. This helps distinguish initial work from ongoing monthly management.

Contract duration is a separate decision from the pricing model. A minimum-term contract can suit a plan that requires continuity, provided the stages and responsibilities are clear. A monthly arrangement without a minimum commitment gives you flexibility. Check the notice period, access to materials and the exit process.

For us, a useful comparison starts with equivalent deliverables. Place proposals side by side and check what you receive, how the fee is calculated and what obligations you take on.

What an advertising management proposal should include

A clear proposal starts with account setup or a review of the existing account. Ask for the campaign structure, billing responsibilities and access to an account held in your business's name. Your business should be able to see campaigns, spending and history, while the agency receives the access needed to manage them.

Next comes tracking: measuring important actions on your website. For an online store, discuss tracked orders and their value. For a service business, define a relevant enquiry and how you will know whether it became a customer. Ask for tracking to be checked so that reporting starts with useful data.

The proposal should also explain how new ads are produced. Who writes the copy, prepares the images and approves the materials? Clarify whether adaptations and testing different messages are included. If you provide the materials, establish what you need to send and how feedback will work.

Ask for weekly optimisation, with adjustments explained using the data. Reporting should connect spending to sales or relevant enquiries and explain what will change next. Impressions and clicks can add context, but decisions should connect to your business goal.

Finally, check who owns each task. A good proposal lets you identify deliverables, responsibilities and evaluation methods without having to interpret vague statements about performance.

Freelancer, agency or an in-house PPC specialist?

If you are asking how much a PPC specialist costs, start with the role you need. Are you looking for someone to manage existing campaigns, or do you also need strategy, copy, creative materials and website improvements? Compare the options by responsibilities and capacity, then by the quoted price.

A freelancer can make sense when you have a clear project and can provide the remaining resources. Direct communication can simplify discussions and approvals. Check availability, working methods and what happens when that person is unavailable. Establish who prepares materials and who resolves tracking issues.

An agency can make sense when you need different skills within the same working relationship. Ask how responsibilities are divided and who will communicate with you. Check whether copy, design, tracking and website work are included in the proposal. These services need concrete descriptions so you can evaluate them.

An in-house specialist can make sense when there is enough recurring work and you need close coordination with your team. Your assessment should include recruitment, onboarding, tools and support for tasks outside that person's expertise.

The choice starts with what your business can support. If you end up coordinating every missing piece, include your own time in the assessment. Ask each option for a plan covering the same goals and responsibilities.

Signs you may be paying too much or too little

You may be paying too much when the cost of the relationship is not justified by the work delivered and its contribution to your business goals. One signal is a report that stops at impressions without explaining what happened to sales or enquiries. Ask for the connection between spending, results and proposed changes for the next period.

Lack of account access is another signal to investigate. You should be able to see where the money goes and which campaigns are running. If you cannot verify the data, evaluating the service or continuing with another provider becomes difficult. Clarify access and account ownership before signing.

A long contract without results, clear stages or a corrective plan deserves reassessment. Ask what has been tested, what has been learned and what comes next. Look for answers based on data and clear responsibility for the issues identified.

You may be paying too little for the workload you expect if important activities are left uncovered. Check whether tracking, new ads, weekly optimisation and reporting are included. Read a low price alongside the exact service list.

With us, evaluation starts with concrete questions about the account, offer and results. Look for the same clarity in any proposal: what gets done, who does it and how you can check.

How to calculate whether an agency is worth it

For the basic comparison, use this formula: total cost = advertising budget + agency fee. Calculate both components for the same period. If you also pay a setup fee or separately billed services, add them to the assessment for the period in which you pay them. This shows the full expense your results need to support.

Next, compare that cost with the margin remaining from sales associated with the campaigns. Establish what remains after product costs and other variable selling costs, before advertising and management. Revenue tells you how much you sold. Available margin tells you how much you can spend to generate those sales.

ROAS compares revenue attributed to ads with the budget spent on advertising platforms. You can find the explanation in our ROAS guide. To assess profitability, add the agency fee separately and account for your margin. Also check whether reported orders match the orders in your store.

If you sell services, follow the journey from enquiry to customer and collected revenue. Speak with the team handling enquiries to establish which were relevant and which became sales.
