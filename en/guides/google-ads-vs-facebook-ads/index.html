Google Ads or Facebook Ads: which should you choose for your business?

To The Sky · Updated 24 November 2026

Choose Google Ads if people already search for what you sell, and Facebook Ads if you need to draw their attention to your offer. Google captures existing demand, while Facebook and Instagram help you create demand through products, demonstrations, and relevant messages. At our agency, the starting point is how your customer buys, followed by your budget and the outcome you can measure.

Google Ads vs Facebook Ads: a comparison for small and medium businesses. The difference in intent mainly refers to Google Search and social media advertising.

Google captures existing demand. Meta helps you create it.

When someone searches for an emergency plumber, you do not need to explain why they need one. The problem already exists. Your ad needs to show that you can help, where you work, and how to contact you. This is where Google Search makes sense: ads that appear in search results.

On Facebook and Instagram, the starting point is different. Someone may be browsing photos or watching a video without looking for a provider. Your ad needs to earn their attention and show a need they recognise. Meta is the company behind Facebook and Instagram; here, we use the name to refer to advertising on those platforms.

Imagine you sell custom furniture. On Google, you can respond to a search for fitted kitchens. On Instagram, you can show how you turned an awkward space into a practical kitchen. The offer stays the same, but the reason someone pays attention changes.

At our agency, this is the first question: is your customer already looking for the solution, or do they need to discover it? The distinction does not cover every campaign. Google Ads also includes visual advertising, and Meta can reach people who already know you. Still, it is a useful starting point when deciding where to advertise.

When should you start with Google Ads?

Google Ads is worth considering when people can clearly describe what they want to buy. That might be a local service, a course, a familiar product, or help with a specific problem. Relevant searches need to exist for your location and offer. A suitable industry does not automatically make every search worth paying for.

In the accounts we manage, we look at the connection between the search, the ad, and the page someone visits. If they search for a particular service, the page should explain that service. It should show what they get, where it is available, and what to do next. A general page can leave too many questions unanswered.

For Matter of Care, a home care service, Google Ads generated steady family inquiries at ~€15 per contact. For Expert English, an English language school, the results were steady enrollment inquiries at an average of ~$16 per inquiry, with costs from $6.67 on Performance Max. Performance Max is a Google campaign type that can distribute ads across several of Google's advertising spaces.

These results show what we achieved in those particular accounts. They are not an estimate for your business. Your offer, competition, website page, and handling of inquiries all affect the outcome.

When should you choose Facebook and Instagram Ads?

Facebook and Instagram are worth testing when you can quickly show why your offer is useful. A product demonstration, a visible transformation, or a simple explanation can start a conversation. You do not need to sell something spectacular. You need to make the benefit easy for the right person to understand.

For services, an ad can begin with a familiar situation. Show the problem, explain how you help, and suggest a natural next step: a conversation, a quote request, or an appointment. If the message promises too much or hides important conditions, you may receive inquiries that never become customers.

For Eva Car Mats, a car mat business in Ireland, Meta campaigns generated over 1,000 WhatsApp inquiries at ~€0.57 each, from €576. These were WhatsApp inquiries, not confirmed orders. That distinction matters when you estimate what advertising could produce for your business.

At our agency, we do not only ask whether an ad gets attention. We want to know whether the person understands the product and can easily continue the conversation. For a business in Romania, we check which materials are available, who answers messages, and what information needs collecting. A good ad also needs a clear process after the first contact. Otherwise, the interest you generate can disappear before you get to make an offer.

Which platform costs less? Look beyond the click.

A cheap click does not tell you whether an ad is worthwhile. You might pay little for visitors who leave immediately, or more for someone who becomes a customer. Cost per click is the average amount spent for a click on an ad. It is a useful indicator, but it cannot replace the business outcome.

Across 12 Meta accounts billed in RON that we managed over the last 90 days, cost per click was RON 0.40-2.62 in 11 of the 12 accounts, while cost per 1,000 impressions was RON 10-32. Cost per 1,000 impressions shows what you pay for ad displays, including repeat displays to the same person. These are observations from our accounts, not guaranteed rates for the Romanian market.

For services, go further: what does a relevant inquiry cost, and how many inquiries become customers? A relevant inquiry comes from someone who fits your service, location, and conditions. For shops, track orders and their value, then check what remains after costs.

At our agency, a Google Ads vs Facebook Ads comparison makes sense when we use the same final outcome. A Google click and a Meta form submission do not represent the same thing. If the cheaper platform brings unsuitable inquiries, the apparent saving can disappear into the time your team spends handling them.

Running an online shop? Check revenue and margin.

For an online shop, the choice starts with your products and how people buy them. If customers search by product name or category, Google can bring visitors who already have an idea of what they want. If the product needs to be seen in use, Meta can support discovery and explain differences that a name alone cannot convey.

For Regal Pet, an online shop, ROAS increased from 8.2x to ~13x across the Google Ads account, with peaks of 14-18x on Performance Max. ROAS is the ratio of revenue attributed to ads to the amount spent on advertising. The result across the whole account and a campaign's peaks are different things, so we present them separately.

ROAS is not profit. Revenue also needs to cover products, delivery, returns, and other expenses. A result that works for one shop may be insufficient for another if their margins differ. At our agency, any discussion about increasing the budget needs to account for these costs.

Before choosing a platform, check whether your product page answers buying questions. Are dimensions, compatibility, delivery, and return conditions clear? Do the photos show what the customer receives? The ad brings someone to the product, but the page needs to help them decide. Missing information can cost you orders regardless of which platform brought the visitor.

If Google brings no inquiries, when should you change direction?

A platform that looks suitable in theory may not work for your offer and situation. That does not mean you should move the budget immediately. First, check whether the ads reach suitable people, whether the page works, and whether inquiries are recorded correctly. Otherwise, you risk changing platforms without fixing the actual problem.

For Cleanix, a cleaning business in Chicago, Google Ads was not generating inquiries. We moved the budget to Meta with instant forms and received inquiries at ~$12 each from the first month. An instant form lets someone submit their contact details within Facebook or Instagram without visiting your website.

This case shows why we do not choose a platform based on industry alone. Cleaning is a service people can search for, but moving to Meta generated inquiries in this account. That does not mean every cleaning company should make the same move. For a company in Romania, the conditions need checking separately.

After the change, the next question is what happens to those inquiries. Do people respond? Are they within the service area? Do they want the service being offered? At our agency, the number of forms is the beginning of the evaluation. Keeping or increasing the budget should also depend on appointments, customers, and the value of the work won.

When does combining Google Ads and Facebook Ads make sense?

The platforms can complement each other when a customer needs several interactions before buying. Someone might discover your product in an Instagram video, then search for your company name on Google. Or they might first visit your website through a search and return after seeing a demonstration on Facebook.

Combining them makes sense when you can clearly explain each platform's role. Meta can introduce the product and why it matters. Google Search can respond to relevant searches. Remarketing means advertising to people who have already interacted with your business, and it can support their return when the necessary conditions for using it are in place.

However, more platforms mean more things to prepare and monitor. You need suitable materials, clear pages, and a consistent way to check sales or inquiries. If your budget is limited, splitting it can make each approach harder to evaluate. At our agency, we want a specific reason for every campaign.

Pay attention to reporting, too. The same order can appear in both platforms' reports because each may attribute a role to its own ad. Do not automatically add reported revenues together as though they represent separate sales. Compare the reports with actual orders and track the overall business result, beyond the figures displayed in each account.

Where should you advertise if you need to choose now?

Start with a simple question: what does your customer do before buying from you? If they actively search for a provider or product, first assess the opportunity in Google Search. If your offer is easier to understand when you show it, start by evaluating Facebook and Instagram. Use customer behaviour as the starting point, then check whether the choice works in practice.

Choose a clear offer for the first test. Explain who it is for, what it includes, and what happens next. Decide what counts as a result: an order, an appointment, or a relevant inquiry. A conversion is the action you track after someone interacts with your ad. A button click and a new customer are conversions with very different values.

Prepare the part your team handles, too. Who answers messages? What information do they ask for? Where do they record whether someone became a customer? Without these answers, you can see activity in the account without knowing what it produced for the business.

At To The Sky, we connect the platform choice to your offer, the page people visit, and the outcome you want. If you would like to discuss your business, tell us what you sell, where you operate, and how you currently get customers. From there, we can assess where to start and what needs preparing before you spend the budget.

In short

Choose Google Ads when there is existing demand to capture, and Facebook Ads when your offer needs to be discovered and understood. Combine them when each has a clear role in the buying process. Judge the result by relevant inquiries, customers, and sales that cover your costs.
