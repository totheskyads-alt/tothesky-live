Where to start and which guide will help you decide

What online advertising means for a small business

For a small business, online advertising should start with a simple question: what do you want someone to do after seeing your offer? They might buy from your store, complete a form or message you on WhatsApp. That choice matters because your message, landing page and tracking need to support the same action. Otherwise, your account can look busy without showing you what it contributes to the business.

Google Ads helps you reach people who are already searching for what you sell. Facebook and Instagram help you create demand by introducing your offer to people who may not have started with a search. TikTok can play a role for products that work well in short videos. The right channel depends on how your customers buy.

That means online advertising for small businesses does not begin with choosing a favourite platform. It begins with the offer. What do you sell, who is it useful for and what does someone need to understand before taking the next step? If the answer is unclear, the advert will carry that same uncertainty. We discuss these points before choosing a campaign.

At To The Sky, we are a young, dynamic team based in Malta, working with advertising and websites. We consider the advert alongside the page visitors reach and the way the business handles enquiries. An order and a completed form need different assessments. In the accounts we manage, we track the outcome that matters to each business.

This guide is your starting point. It shows how your channel, budget, website and tracking fit together, then directs you to the detailed explanation you need. If choosing a platform is your first question, start with our comparison of Google Ads and Facebook Ads.

Throughout the process, keep your commercial objective in view. Online promotion for a small business makes sense when you can explain what you want to achieve and how you will assess the outcome. Click numbers can help with analysis, but decisions need to connect to orders, relevant enquiries and the money left over.

What to check before paying for your first click

Before sending people to your website, follow the same path a customer will take. Open the page on your phone, read the offer and try to place an order or submit an enquiry. Check where the message goes and whether the person responsible for replying can see it. We do this before launching adverts, alongside checking website speed and WhatsApp functionality.

Do not stop at a confirmation message appearing on the form. Check that the enquiry actually reaches the business. For Eva Car Mats, the car mat store in Ireland, we first repaired the website, where forms were not sending anything. Then, using Meta instant forms, we generated over 1.000 WhatsApp leads at ~0,57 € each, from 576 € invested. This was the outcome of that project, not an estimate for every business.

The next check is tracking. You need to know which action you are measuring and how it appears in the tools you use. We check Google Tag Manager, Consent Mode and the pixel according to the channels selected. The aim is to connect spending to the intended outcome while respecting the choice visitors make in the cookie banner.

Then we discuss margins. If you sell products, consider what remains after the costs of an order and how much you can allocate to acquiring it. If you sell services, look at the connection between a relevant enquiry and the job or contract that might follow. A submitted enquiry does not automatically mean a completed sale.

You can organise the checks like this:

1. Test the page and its main action from the customer's perspective. 2. Confirm that the form, order or message reaches the right place. 3. Check tracking and how tags behave after a choice is made in the banner. 4. Clarify your margin and the outcome worth paying for.

Only after these checks should you choose the offer to promote. If the page needs work, address it before launch. In our approach to website creation, the page and the commercial objective need to be considered together. Advertising needs a working path through to an order or enquiry.

Which channel should you start with: Google, Facebook or TikTok?

Choosing a channel starts with customer behaviour. If someone already knows what they need and expresses that need in a search, Google is worth considering. If a product needs to be discovered, demonstrated or explained before interest develops, Facebook and Instagram may have a useful role. We do not choose solely on the basis of which platform the business owner uses personally.

Google captures existing demand. For a service business, the question is whether the offer and landing page answer the search that brought the visitor there. For an online store, the discussion may include Search, Shopping and Performance Max. The specific choice needs to connect to what you sell and the outcome you can measure, rather than simply to the availability of a campaign type.

Facebook and Instagram help you create demand. Here, the message needs to make your offer easy to understand for someone who was not searching for it beforehand. Show the product, explain its use and maintain the connection between the advert and its destination. If the advert presents a particular offer, people should recognise it when they reach the page.

TikTok enters the discussion when a product works well in short videos. Consider whether you can show it in use and whether your material explains why your audience might care. Do not launch simply because you want a presence on another platform. Read about our approach to TikTok advertising if the format suits your product.

We start with a single channel and a clear objective. We add another channel when the first is stable and each can have a defined role. For example, a discussion about combining channels should distinguish between capturing an existing search and presenting an offer to people who are not yet looking for it. Running adverts in several places does not, by itself, explain the outcome.

For your initial decision, use our Google Ads or Facebook Ads guide. Consider it alongside your offer, budget and intended action. Choose the channel for which you can explain the customer's path from first contact to an order or relevant enquiry.

How much budget do you need for online advertising?

Budgeting starts with separating costs. Money spent on the platform and the fee for managing the advertising are different expenses. When comparing options, ask for them to be presented separately. Otherwise, an apparently attractive total can be difficult to assess because you do not know how much goes into adverts or what work the agreement includes.

Across the 7 online stores with Google Ads accounts in RON that we manage (26 August - 23 November 2026): cost per click between 0,82 and 1,99 RON, cost per order between ~20 and ~53 RON, median cost per order ~36 RON. The context includes Search, Shopping, Performance Max. You can find the explanations in our guide to how much Google Ads costs.

In the Meta accounts we manage: 10-32 RON per 1.000 impressions, link clicks at 0,40-2,62 RON, and, for online stores, cost per order of 18-80 RON, with a median of ~57 RON. These are results from our accounts, not fixed rates. See our Facebook and Instagram advertising cost guide for this part of your planning.

According to Brig.ro: Facebook/Instagram management through an agency costs 1.000-5.000 RON per month, separate from a minimum advertising budget of 2.100-9.000 RON per month; Google Ads has a minimum budget of 2.100-3.000 RON per month plus management. These figures are not our offer and do not automatically determine the right budget for your business. We discuss them in our guide to marketing agency costs.

Do not turn the ranges into a promise. Comparing the Google and Meta medians is not enough to choose a channel either. These are results from particular accounts, while your decision needs to reflect your offer, margins and customers' buying behaviour. A cost per order only makes sense when you know the order's economic value to your business.

Set the budget alongside your objective and management costs. Keep clear what you can sustain and what you need to learn from the campaign. If the available amount does not support the proposed plan, narrow the offer or the starting channel. We prefer a starting point we can explain and assess clearly.

How to measure orders, enquiries and ROAS

Tracking starts with the action that matters to your business. For an online store, you track orders and their value. For services, you track relevant enquiries and what happens after they are submitted. Before reading a report, clarify what each displayed result represents. A page visit, an enquiry and an order should not be treated as the same thing.

For Google, Consent Mode v2 communicates the choice made in the cookie banner to Google tags. It does not replace the banner. Meta and TikTok are handled separately. These distinctions matter when checking an implementation because the presence of a banner does not, by itself, explain how every tool behaves. Find the explanation in our guide to Consent Mode v2 and tracking.

For Meta, checks take place in Events Manager. Standard events include ViewContent, AddToCart, Purchase and Lead. Purchase needs a value and currency, and Conversions API is implemented with deduplication. The pixel starts after marketing consent. Follow our guide to installing the Meta pixel when preparing or checking this part of your website.

ROAS = sales from advertising / advertising spend. Example: 1.000 RON spent, 5.000 RON in sales = ROAS 5. The formula helps relate reported revenue to the amount spent. However, the ROAS shown in the platform is not profit. Your minimum threshold comes from your margin, so you also need to consider the business's costs.

Our ROAS guide with calculator lets you continue this assessment. The useful question is not simply whether the report looks good, but whether the outcome leaves enough money after costs. For services, add feedback about enquiries: what people want, whether they fit your offer and whether those conversations lead to sales.

We check tracking before launch and revisit it when the data does not align with what the business sees. A report should support a specific decision: maintain the direction, change the offer or fix a problem. If you cannot explain what a displayed result means, clarify the tracking before judging the campaign on that number alone.

How to approach the first months of advertising

The first months need a clear direction, rather than an expectation that every report will immediately confirm the plan. Start with your chosen offer, a single channel and the outcome you want. Then check whether people reach the right destination, whether actions are tracked and whether the business recognises the results in the orders or enquiries received. These checks provide context for any budget decision.

At the start, maintain the connection between the advert and what happens after the click. For a store, discuss orders and margins. For services, ask for feedback on enquiry quality. If people ask for something you do not sell, revisit the message. If the offer is understood but the form does not work, fix the website before asking more of the campaign.

Record the reason for changes. When you modify the offer, page or message, state which question you are trying to answer. This keeps the next discussion from becoming a vague impression that the report looks better or worse. You can connect the outcome to a decision and decide whether to maintain, adjust or stop that direction.

One example from our work is Regal Pet, an online pet store. With Google Ads, using Shopping and Performance Max: ROAS went from 8,2x to ~13x across the account, with over 270.000 RON in monthly sales and a budget of ~3.400 € per month (~6% of revenue). These are the results of that account. We do not use them as a timetable or a promise for another business starting out.

Another channel comes after the first is stable and you can explain the new channel's role. Ask which need it addresses: existing demand or discovery of the offer. Without a clear answer, expansion can make assessment harder to follow. Additional budget should be discussed alongside the commercial outcome you want to achieve.

Our conversations stay focused on orders, relevant enquiries and the money left over. We do not judge success solely by clicks or turn a case study into an expected result for every client. The first months should give you an increasingly clear picture of what is worth continuing in your business.

Should you choose an agency, a freelancer or manage advertising yourself?

The choice depends on what you can manage within the business and where you need help. You can work alone, with a freelancer or with an agency, but responsibilities need to be clear. Who prepares the offer? Who checks the website and tracking? Who follows up on enquiries? Without these answers, campaign management alone can leave important problems falling between the people involved.

If you manage advertising yourself, take responsibility for both account decisions and checks outside the account. You need to connect the message, the page and the commercial outcome. Before starting, consider whether you have the capacity to follow what happens and resolve obstacles. This option makes sense when you can sustain the work alongside the day-to-day running of your business.

If you work with a freelancer, clarify exactly what the arrangement includes. Discuss adverts, materials, the website, tracking and reporting. Do not assume everything is included simply because the service is called management. The same questions apply to an agency. The type of provider does not replace the need for a clear agreement about the actual work.

When assessing an agency, check whether it understands how you make money. Ask it to explain what it would track for your business and how it would connect its work to sales or enquiries. Ask for explained examples, a sample report and the terms of the relationship. Our guide to choosing a marketing agency helps you structure the discussion without relying solely on the presentation.

Separate the advertising budget from the agency fee and ask what each covers. Check account ownership too. With us, accounts remain in the client's name. You should know where your adverts run, how you access them and who can make changes. These points are part of organising the working relationship, not just technical details.

At To The Sky, we start by understanding the offer, website, tracking and margins. The right arrangement is one in which you understand what is being done and why. If the explanations do not connect to orders or relevant enquiries for your business, keep asking questions before making your choice.

Which mistakes make advertising decisions harder?

A common mistake is choosing the advert before clarifying the offer. If you cannot say who you sell to and which action you want, settings will not resolve that gap. Start with the message the customer needs to understand. Then check that the page continues the same explanation and lets them take the requested step without searching elsewhere for essential information.

Another mistake is judging a campaign solely by clicks. A click shows an interaction, but the commercial question remains open: did an order or relevant enquiry follow? In the accounts we manage, we use these figures for analysis, then return to the business outcome. We do not determine a campaign's value simply because the report shows active traffic.

It is just as easy to read ROAS as profit. The ratio between sales and advertising spend does not replace a margin calculation. If that distinction is unclear, return to our explanation of ROAS and the minimum threshold. Assess the budget alongside the money remaining after costs, rather than only the sales value displayed by the platform.

You can also make assessment harder by launching on every channel without clear roles. We start with a single channel and objective, then add another once the first is stable. That allows us to explain why each campaign exists. A presence on Google, Meta and TikTok is not, by itself, a plan for your business.

Another trap is treating tracking as a box to tick. The cookie banner, Consent Mode and pixel need to be checked within the actual implementation. Consent Mode v2 does not replace the banner, and Meta and TikTok are handled separately. If the reported results do not match what you observe in the business, revisit those checks before drawing conclusions about the offer.

Finally, do not adopt another business's result as an automatic target. Case studies and ranges from our accounts provide context. Your plan starts with your own offer, margins and capacity to handle orders or enquiries. These reference points help you make decisions you can explain.

What to do when your account is restricted or costs rise

When a problem appears, start by establishing exactly what has happened. Have the adverts stopped running? Is the website receiving no enquiries? Does the cost per order no longer make sense against your margin? These are different situations and need separate analysis. Changing the budget before understanding the obstacle does not tell you whether you have resolved the cause or merely changed the spend.

If a Facebook advertising account is restricted, check Account Quality and request a review. Meta says reviews are usually completed in approximately 48 hours. This information is attributed to Meta; it is not a deadline we guarantee. Do not create new accounts to bypass the restriction. Follow our guide to a restricted Facebook advertising account for this situation.

If cost is the issue, first check which outcome you are measuring. Are you talking about a click, an order or a relevant enquiry? Cost per click cannot answer the profitability question on its own. Consider orders, their value and your margin together. For services, check whether enquiries are suitable and what happens after they reach the business.

Then follow the customer's path again. Test the form, checkout and WhatsApp where relevant. Check whether the page presents the offer shown in the advert and whether tracking records the intended action. If the business receives enquiries but the report does not reflect them, clarify the tracking. If the form does not send, repair it before judging the advert by the lack of enquiries.

You can also revisit the figures in our Google Ads cost guide or Meta advertising cost guide, without treating them as mandatory limits. Results from our accounts provide context. The decision to continue or adjust still depends on your business economics and the quality of the outcome.

For us, the next step needs a clear reason. We fix the identified problem, check what the data shows and discuss whether the offer or campaign needs to change. When you do not yet have an explanation, keep the question open. A useful conclusion needs to connect what you see in the account with what happens in the business.

Your plan for the next 30 days

Use the next 30 days as an organising framework, not as a promise of results. The aim is to arrive at a clear offer, a working customer journey and an advertising decision you can assess. If you discover a website or tracking problem, resolving it belongs in the plan before expanding your advertising.

1. Choose the offer and objective. Write down what you are promoting, who you are addressing and what you want them to do. For a store, that might be an order; for services, a relevant enquiry. Keep the same direction in the advertising message and the landing page.

2. Check the website. Test the form, checkout and WhatsApp where relevant. Confirm that messages reach the business and that the offer is easy to understand on a phone. Include speed in your checks. Record specific problems and resolve them before sending paid traffic.

3. Check tracking and margins. Clarify the intended action, Google Tag Manager, Consent Mode and the pixel according to your channel. Establish how you will connect the outcome to business costs. For orders, discuss ROAS alongside margins. For services, establish how you will assess enquiry quality.

4. Choose the channel and budget. Start with how your customers buy. Separate advertising spend from management fees and choose an amount you can sustain. We begin with a single channel and objective so the direction can be explained clearly.

5. Launch and track the commercial outcome. Check whether the data connects to the orders or enquiries received. Record what you change and why. Add another channel when the first is stable and you can explain the new channel's role in your business plan.

If you would like us to work through these checks with you, at To The Sky we start with a free website and advertising audit. We are a young, dynamic team based in Malta, and the accounts remain in your name. The discussion starts with your business: what you sell, what works now and what needs to be clarified before you invest further.
