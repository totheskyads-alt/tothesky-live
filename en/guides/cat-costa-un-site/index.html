How much does a business website cost in 2026? Real prices in Romania

To The Sky · 25 November 2026

Publicly listed prices for a business website in Romania range from around RON 990 to more than €2,500. Adapted template offers start at RON 990, include a RON 1,799 package, or start at €500. For design built from scratch, listed offers start at RON 3,900, €2,000 and €2,500. These prices were published by several Romanian web agencies and checked in September 2026. Below, we explain what each price level covers and how to compare offers for your business.

1. Website costs at a glance

Listed prices for a business website start at around RON 990 and extend beyond €2,500. The difference becomes clearer when you compare page counts, the design approach and the work included. A starting price tells you where the discussion begins. The deliverables tell you what you are buying.

Among the adapted template offers checked, one costs RON 1,799 for up to 5 pages. The client supplies the copy, and the package includes one round of changes. For design built from scratch, one offer starts at RON 3,900 for 5 pages, with 2 revisions and delivery in 14 working days. These details make the comparison useful.

Other listed prices start at €500 for a template or PRO theme, and at €2,000 and €2,500 for design built from scratch. These are separate offers. Do not assume they include the same services simply because they describe the same type of website.

Some prices exclude VAT or come from businesses that are not VAT registered, so check what the final quote includes. When comparing website costs, use the same requirements for each provider. Ask about page counts, responsibility for content and included changes. This connects each price to a clear outcome for your business.

2. Adapted template or design built from scratch?

An adapted template starts with an existing visual structure. You choose a suitable base for your business, then agree how your branding, copy and images will fit into it. This can suit a straightforward business presentation, services that are easy to explain and content that is ready to use. The quote should specify how much adaptation is included.

At this level, the checked prices include a starting point of RON 990, the RON 1,799 package and an offer from €500. The RON 1,799 package covers up to 5 pages, copy supplied by you and one round of changes. The offer from €500 specifies a template or PRO theme.

Design built from scratch allows the appearance and structure to be shaped around how your business needs to present itself. It becomes relevant when services need different explanations, the enquiry process has specific requirements or your visual identity needs detailed treatment. Ask how the design will be approved before implementation.

Listed offers here start at RON 3,900, €2,000 and €2,500. The first includes 5 pages, 2 revisions and delivery in 14 working days. To choose, write down what visitors need to understand and what action you want them to take. Then check whether the proposed approach supports that journey.

3. What the price includes and what costs extra

The creation price should be tied to a clear list of deliverables. Ask for the pages, design, implementation and contact features to be written into the quote. If you need a form, specify what information it collects and where enquiries go. If you want WhatsApp contact, agree how visitors reach the conversation and what message they can send.

Discuss content at the start. Who writes the copy? Who selects and prepares the images? You can supply the materials or ask for their production to be quoted separately. The checked RON 1,799 package requires copy from the client. That means you need content ready for the agreed pages.

Translations are another point to clarify. For a multilingual website, list the languages, the pages to be translated and who checks the wording. Discuss annual domain registration and hosting, maintenance and updates separately. If you need online selling, request an online store quote, which belongs to a different price level.

4. Annual costs after launch

After launch, your website budget continues with annual domain registration and hosting. The domain is the address you share with customers. Hosting is the service that runs the website. Ask who manages them, when they renew and how you receive payment information. Keep these details somewhere you can access.

Discuss maintenance and updates separately from the initial build. Ask which tasks are included, how you report a problem and who checks functionality after changes. A maintenance plan should describe specific activities. Clarify content changes too: updating a service, replacing a photograph and adding a page may have different terms.

Plan for the materials you will need over time. When your business offering changes, you may need new copy, images or translations. Agree who prepares them and who adds them to the website. This helps you organise updates around actual business changes and keep the information useful for customers.

Before accepting the project, ask for a clear breakdown of the initial build, recurring services and work requested as needed. Note what you can update yourself and what requires the technical team. Check who receives renewal notices as well. Your ongoing budget becomes easier to track, and responsibility remains clear for each service you use.

5. Business website or online store?

A business website explains what you offer and helps visitors contact you. It can present services, products, examples of work and answers to customer questions. Its main action might be a quote request, a WhatsApp conversation or a form submission. The choice starts with how you sell.

An online store adds the purchasing process. You need to define the products, how orders are placed, payment and delivery. These requirements change the project and move it to a different price level. Request a separate quote based on the features you need. The business website prices in this guide do not describe an ecommerce package.

If your sales process begins with a conversation, an enquiry focused website can support it. You can ask visitors for the details needed to prepare a quote and continue the conversation directly. If you want people to select products and complete their orders on the website, define the full purchasing journey from the start.

For Transfer Confort Line, we built a multilingual website with WhatsApp bookings. It is an example of a website organised around a clear action. For your business, first decide whether the goal is to receive enquiries or completed online orders. That decision helps you request the right proposal.

6. What your website quote should contain

A useful quote lets you check what you will receive before the project starts. Begin with the pages: their names, the purpose of each and the languages they will appear in. Then cover copy and images. Specify who prepares them, who approves them and when they must be supplied. These details help you organise your part of the work.

Revisions need the same clarity. Ask how many rounds are included, when you should submit feedback and what counts as a round of changes. Agree the delivery deadline and the materials needed to meet it. If you want additional pages or features, ask how these are assessed and added to the project.

For technical delivery, request clear criteria for speed and mobile checks. For basic SEO, ask for the included tasks, such as setting page titles and descriptions. For tracking, define which actions will be measured and which accounts will hold the data. Forms and contact links should be checked before launch.

The quote should specify who owns the domain and how you receive access to the website, hosting and measurement accounts. 7. How to choose a budget for your business

Choose your budget based on what the website needs to do for your business. Write down what you sell, who you serve and what you want someone to do after landing on a page. For a straightforward presentation, check whether an adapted template meets your requirements. For a specific structure and a design shaped around it, compare offers built from scratch.

If the website needs to generate enquiries from ads, pay attention to speed and the contact page. Visitors should quickly find the service promised in the ad, the information they need and a clear way to contact you. Ask for the form to be checked on mobile and define which actions tracking should measure. Discuss these requirements before design begins.

Consider the website and promotion together. For campaign budgeting, read our guide to Facebook advertising costs. Then separate the website build, ongoing costs and promotion in your plan. You can decide what is needed now and what can be added later, based on the enquiries received.
