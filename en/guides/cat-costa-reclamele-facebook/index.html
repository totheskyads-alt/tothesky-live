How much do Facebook and Instagram ads cost in 2026?

Mihai Jdovanu, To The Sky · Updated 24 November 2026

In the Meta accounts we manage, Facebook and Instagram ads cost between 10 and 32 RON (Romanian lei) per 1,000 impressions, while link clicks cost between 0.40 and 2.62 RON in 11 of 12 accounts. In 8 of 9 online shops, the cost per order was between 18 and 80 RON, with a median of approximately 57 RON. These are results from our accounts, not fixed prices or a guaranteed estimate for your business.

What do CPM, cost per click and cost per result mean?

When you search for Facebook advertising costs, you can find very different answers because people are measuring different things. Some mean impressions. Others mean website visits, enquiries or orders. We start the conversation with a question: what do you want your ads to achieve?

CPM means the cost of displaying your ad 1,000 times. Impressions do not mean different people, because the same person can see your ad more than once. This metric tells you what it costs to appear in front of your audience, but it does not tell you whether you sold anything.

CPC means cost per click, and our table measures link clicks. A click shows that someone pressed the link in your ad. It does not confirm that the page fully loaded or that the person bought something.

Cost per result means what you spent on the action you are tracking, such as an order or an enquiry. You calculate it by dividing your spend by the number of results attributed to your ads.

In the accounts we manage, we look at how these metrics connect. A cheap click may bring someone who leaves immediately. A more expensive click may bring a suitable buyer. When comparing Facebook advertising costs, compare the same action and check what happens afterwards. Your business also needs money left over after the sale.

Why do advertising costs vary so much?

Facebook and Instagram do not charge a fixed price for each customer they bring you. Ads compete for people's attention, and the cost depends on the audience, competition and how people respond to your message. In our accounts, differences appear throughout the journey from impression to purchase.

Your sector matters. A straightforward product and a business service can involve very different buying decisions. The business services account paying approximately 5.48 RON per click needs to be assessed against its contract values and the enquiries it receives. We do not judge it solely against the median click cost.

Your offer matters just as much. People need to understand what they get, what they pay and why they should buy from you. The ad can attract attention, but the page needs to continue the same message. Unclear pricing, delivery information or ordering steps can lose a visitor after you have paid for the click.

Seasonality also changes costs. Around Black Friday and in December, competition can make impressions more expensive. That does not automatically make every order more expensive, because people's willingness to buy can change too.

Your chosen audience influences the outcome. We assess the audience, message, offer and page together. Otherwise, you risk changing the ad when the actual problem sits somewhere else.

What minimum budget do you need for Facebook and Instagram?

Meta's accepted daily minimum in a RON account is 4.88 RON per day, while in a euro account it is €0.92 per day. This is the technical minimum. It does not mean the amount is a useful budget for your objective or enough to assess sales performance.

A useful budget starts with the result you want and its estimated cost. During the learning phase, the system explores how to deliver your ads. Under Meta's official rule, an ad set usually completes this phase after approximately 50 results for its chosen optimisation event within 7 days. An ad set is a group of ads sharing audience, budget and optimisation settings. Reference: Meta, about the learning phase.

Example calculation: if an order costs approximately 57 RON, then 50 orders require approximately 2,850 RON per week. This is a planning reference, assuming that cost holds. It is not a requirement for getting started.

If that budget is unavailable, we discuss a simpler structure and slower learning. We may consider optimising for an earlier action, such as adding to basket or making an enquiry, where it fits the business. However, the system will seek that action, not necessarily buyers. We then check whether those actions lead to sales.

How do you calculate what you can afford to pay for a customer?

Before choosing a budget, you need to know what you can spend on an order without using up all the money left from the sale. We start with margin. Margin is the share of the selling price remaining after the costs included in your calculation, so you need to be clear about what you have already deducted.

Hypothetical example: you sell a product for 200 RON with a 40% margin. That leaves 80 RON before advertising. Your cost per order must stay below 80 RON to avoid losing money on the first order, provided all other relevant costs are already included. At 80 RON, advertising would consume the entire available margin.

Check what your calculation covers. If packaging, payment fees, delivery costs you absorb or returns are excluded, deduct them separately. The agency fee also needs to be covered. Your actual advertising limit may therefore be lower.

An order does not automatically mean a new customer. If you want the cost of acquiring a new customer, separate new buyers from orders placed by returning customers.

Repeat purchases can change the calculation, but we do not assume they will happen simply to make the result look better. We use the business's actual history. Until you have that evidence, a cautious estimate starts with what you genuinely keep from the first order.

How much does an enquiry cost for a service business?

An enquiry, also called a lead, is a contact from someone expressing interest in your offer. It is not a confirmed sale. In the accounts we manage, a beauty salon paid approximately 8.8 RON per enquiry, a swimming school approximately 45 RON and a services company approximately 230 RON.

These are different services with different values and buying processes. We therefore do not turn them into a universal price for enquiries.

We also have public examples from other markets. Eva Car Mats, which sells car mats in Ireland, received over 1,000 WhatsApp enquiries at approximately €0.57 each, from €576 spent. Cleanix, a cleaning business in Chicago, received 37 enquiries at approximately $12 each, from approximately $450, in its first month. These results are not estimates for the Romanian market.

Instant forms, completed directly inside Facebook or Instagram, and WhatsApp conversations can reduce the steps between seeing an ad and making contact. This can make enquiries cheaper, but an easy submission does not automatically mean a suitable customer.

We check whether the person responds, lives within the service area, needs the service offered and proceeds to an appointment or contract. We also follow what happens after the enquiry, because a list of cheap contacts can produce very few sales.

What should you expect in your first month of advertising?

The first month is a period for testing your offer, messages and the path to an order or enquiry. In the accounts we manage, we consider what the spend can teach us, alongside what the business can afford. Testing needs a clear direction and an agreed budget.

During the first 2-3 weeks, allow room for learning and fluctuations in the figures. This is a working window for initial assessment, not Meta's officially guaranteed learning phase duration. The system needs relevant results, and a small budget or limited order volume can extend the process.

One example in our data is the new supplements shop that paid approximately 551 RON per order in its first months, before optimisation. We keep this expensive case in the analysis because showing only favourable results would give an incomplete picture. It does not mean every new shop will face that cost, or that the cost will automatically fall.

We check whether measurement works, whether people reach the page and where they stop. We then test changes that address a clear problem. We do not keep a weak ad running simply because it is still learning, but we do not judge an entire business after a day either. At the end of the period, you need an evidence-based decision about what to continue, change and stop.

Which mistakes can make Facebook and Instagram ads more expensive?

A common mistake is pressing 'Boost post' without deciding which result you want. Promoting a post can serve a purpose, but likes are not the same as enquiries or orders. We choose the objective according to the action the business needs.

An audience that is too small can restrict delivery and lead to the same people seeing the ad repeatedly. However, a local business should not expand into areas it cannot serve. We check whether the chosen restrictions are necessary.

Using a single ad gives you little to compare. If the message or image does not work, you cannot see which alternative might answer the customer's questions more effectively. Tests need meaningful differences, rather than changes made simply for the sake of changing something.

A slow page can lose visitors after the click. An unclear page can lose them once it loads. Check the mobile experience, the information about your offer and how easily someone can send an enquiry or complete an order.

Without the pixel and accurate measurement, decisions rely on incomplete information. The Meta pixel is a tool that sends the platform information about certain actions on your website. We check the measurement setup and compare reported results with actual orders and enquiries. A figure in the account needs to be understood before it becomes a reason to increase spending.

What does an agency cost, and what do you pay Meta separately?

When you search for Facebook advertising prices, some offers describe management fees alone, while others also include ad spend. Before comparing proposals, ask to see the agency payment separately from the amount that actually goes to Meta.

Your advertising budget is the amount paid to Meta to distribute ads on Facebook and Instagram. The agency payment covers the agreed services. Depending on the arrangement, these may include strategy, campaign setup, performance monitoring and ongoing adjustments. Do not assume every proposal includes the same work.

We discuss responsibilities from the start: who prepares copy, images and video, who checks measurement and who makes website changes. You should also know which information you need to provide, including margins, product availability, enquiry quality and confirmed sales. The agency needs these details to interpret results properly.

This guide does not include an agency rate, because a price without a defined scope of work does not help you compare. Ask for a proposal that makes the services and responsibilities clear.

For your own calculation, add together Meta spend, the agency payment and any other costs required for the advertising. The costs per order in our table describe ad spend. They do not automatically represent the total cost of acquiring a customer. Your business needs to support the complete investment, not just the amount shown in the advertising account.

In short

In our accounts, reference figures are between 0.40 and 2.62 RON per click in 11 of 12 accounts and between 18 and 80 RON per order in 8 of 9 shops, but much more expensive cases also exist. The right budget for you starts with your margin, the result you want and what you can allocate to testing. Include the agency payment, then assess sales and the money left over, not just clicks.
