How to choose a marketing agency without wasting your budget

To The Sky · Updated 24 November 2026

Choose a marketing agency by checking whether it understands how your business makes money, explains what it will do, and connects its work to sales or relevant enquiries. Ask for examples with context, a sample report and the terms of the engagement before signing. We start with a free audit so our proposal reflects what your business needs.

Questions to ask in your first meeting

What does a marketing agency do for your business?

A marketing agency helps you reach suitable customers and move them closer to a purchase. It may work on advertising, your website, content or how you present your offer. Services vary between teams, so ask exactly what the engagement includes. A long list of activities does not, by itself, explain which problem they solve.

If you run a service business, your goal may be enquiries from people in the area you cover. If you run an online shop, you care about orders and what remains after costs. Those differences should change the agency's recommendation. The same package will not automatically suit both businesses.

When you look for an online marketing agency, ask how each service connects to your goal. Why do you need a new page? What should someone understand from the advert? Where are potential customers getting stuck? The explanation should make sense to you even if you have never worked in marketing.

We start with what you sell and what you want to achieve. We work on advertising and websites, but each recommendation needs a concrete reason. Ask any agency to explain what it needs from you as well: information about your offer, approvals, product availability or someone to handle enquiries. For the big picture, read our complete guide to online ads for small businesses.

Does the agency understand how your business makes money?

The first conversation should include questions about your business before recommendations about advertising. What sells most easily? Which products are worth promoting? Who buys, and why do they choose you? How much can you deliver without affecting quality? These answers help the agency understand whether its approach fits how your business operates.

Margin is the difference between revenue from a sale and the costs included in your calculation. Be clear about which costs you have included when discussing it. Revenue can grow without profit moving in the same direction. That is why advertising recommendations need to consider what you actually retain from each sale.

What happens after someone contacts you matters too. Who replies? How do you decide whether an enquiry is suitable? How do you follow up on proposals? Advertising can bring interested people, but the outcome also depends on their experience after that first message. Ask how the agency will use this information.

We carry out a free audit before preparing a proposal. An audit reviews the current situation to identify problems and priorities. When you receive one, look for observations specific to your business. Ask what should change first, why it matters and which missing information would help the agency make a more informed recommendation.

Why does the agency recommend Facebook Ads or another channel?

If you started by searching for a Facebook Ads agency, you probably already have a channel in mind. Facebook Ads are paid advertisements on Facebook, managed through Meta's advertising platform. Still, the service you choose should follow the problem you want to solve. Ask why that channel suits your offer and the way your customers buy.

Ask for a simple explanation of the proposed customer journey. What will someone see in the advert? Where will they land after clicking? What should they do on that page? If the website does not support the promise in the advert, visitors may be left with questions. The agency should be able to assess the advert, page and offer together.

A useful recommendation also explains what will be tested. This might be the message, the image or how you explain your service. Ask which assumption the test examines and what will guide the next decision. Saying “we will try different versions” needs that extra explanation before you can assess the work.

Be cautious about an immediate recommendation to advertise everywhere. Each channel requires materials, management and budget. Ask for priorities and clear reasons for their order. Also ask when the agency would recommend improving your website or offer before spending more on advertising.

How do you assess the agency's experience and team?

A portfolio shows who the agency has worked with, but it does not automatically explain what it did. Choose an example close to your situation and ask for the full story. What was the original problem? What was the team responsible for? What changed? How did it check whether the change helped? Look for a connection between decisions and outcomes.

A case study describes a project, including its problem, the work carried out and the results observed. You need context to judge it. An established shop and a new business start from different positions. Ask what resembles your company and what cannot be compared. A credible answer acknowledges those differences.

Find out who will actually work for you. The person presenting the proposal may have a different role from the person managing your advertising. Clarify who makes decisions, who prepares materials and who you contact when a problem arises. If external contributors are involved, ask how they are coordinated and who is responsible for delivery.

At To The Sky, you work directly with our team, without intermediaries. Look for that same clarity about responsibilities in every team you consider. You do not need to understand every technical detail. You need to know who can explain the work and follow through on your agreement.

How do you know whether advertising delivers useful results?

Agree on what counts as a useful result before advertising starts. For a shop, it might be a confirmed order. For a service business, it might be an enquiry that matches its offer and service area. A conversion is an action you choose to measure, such as a purchase or a submitted form. Not every conversion is automatically a sale.

Ask how the agency checks that these actions are recorded correctly. A form submitted twice should not automatically be interpreted as two different customers. A cancelled order changes the picture too. Ask how the agency compares advertising data with what you see in orders and conversations with potential customers.

We report on sales and enquiries, not likes. That means discussing the quality of enquiries as well as whether they arrived. If people keep asking about a service you do not provide, the people managing your campaigns need to know. Otherwise, they are working with an incomplete picture.

Agree on reporting frequency in writing and ask for a sample before committing. A useful report shows what was spent, which results were recorded and what happens next. It should also explain the limits of the data. When something cannot be verified, you need to know that.

What should you check in the proposal, budget and contract?

Compare proposals by what they include, as well as the total. The agency fee pays for its work, while the advertising budget pays for spending on advertising platforms. Ask for a clear separation between them. Check for other costs involving creative materials, website changes or required tools. This lets you understand what you are comparing.

The proposal should specify what you receive. Does advertising management include copywriting? Who prepares the images? Are changes to the page visitors land on included? If a new requirement appears, who estimates the work and how do you approve it? Broad wording can create different expectations even when both sides have good intentions.

Read the terms covering duration, any minimum commitment, renewal and ending the engagement. Ask for an explanation of anything you do not understand. Find out what happens if missing materials or approvals delay the project. Your responsibilities need to be as clear as the agency's so you can both plan realistically.

Clarify who can change the advertising budget and how additional spending is approved. Also agree on how to raise a problem when it appears. The contract does not need to become a marketing manual. It needs to reflect the promises made in your conversations, so you understand what you are buying and how the engagement works.

Do you keep your accounts when the engagement ends?

Discuss access and handover before the first campaign. Your advertising account, website and measurement tools matter for business continuity. Ask who manages them and which access you retain. Receiving reports does not automatically mean you can log into the accounts or continue working with another team.

With us, the advertising account stays in the client's name. The agency receives access for the work it handles. Admin access lets you manage account users and permissions. Check from the beginning that the appropriate person in your business has this access and knows how to use it when needed.

Separately, clarify which materials you can use after the engagement ends. Ask about copy, images, pages and the source files used to create them. Some resources may have separate usage conditions. Ask for the agreed handover arrangements and usage rights to be described clearly before your business depends on those materials.

Agree on which information will be handed over, in what format and who will coordinate the transfer. Include active campaigns, relevant findings and any unfinished work. Clarify when the agency's access will be removed and who will check permissions. A well-organised engagement allows you to continue operating if you eventually decide to take a different direction.

How do you choose without relying on the sales presentation?

Use the questions in the table in every conversation and write down the answers. Ask for the same basic information: an explained recommendation, a relevant example, a sample report and the terms of the engagement. This helps you compare how each team approaches your problem. A polished presentation helps communication, but your decision needs these details too.

Be cautious about guaranteed sales before the agency understands your business. Ask what supports the promise and which factors could change the outcome. Your offer, competitors, website and sales process all matter. A considered answer explains what the team can control and what it needs to monitor with you.

Also ask what happens when results are poor. Who investigates? What information will they need from you? How do they decide what to change, and when will they tell you? Look for a process you understand. Pressure to sign immediately, unanswered questions about access or a refusal to separate costs need clarification before you commit.

Choose a team you can speak to directly about money, limitations and results. At To The Sky, we work directly with you and start with a free audit before preparing a proposal. Request an audit if you want us to review what may be holding your marketing back. You can assess our recommendation before deciding whether to work with us.

In short

Choose an agency that explains its recommendations, accepts clear responsibilities and measures what matters to your business. Before signing, check the services, costs, account access and exit terms. Request To The Sky's free audit if you want to start with a review of your current situation.
